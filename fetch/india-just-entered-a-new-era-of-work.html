India Just Entered a New Era of Work — And Every Employer and Worker Will Feel the Impact

India’s labour reforms took a major step forward as four consolidated labour codes came into effect on 21 November 2025. These codes modernise rules on wages, industrial relations, social security, and workplace safety. Understand the key changes, benefits, and challenges.

A Complete Breakdown of India’s New Labour Codes Implemented on 21 November 2025

On 21 November 2025, India implemented four major Labour Codes—the Code on Wages (2019), Industrial Relations Code (2020), Code on Social Security (2020), and Occupational Safety, Health & Working Conditions (OSHWC) Code (2020). These replace 29 earlier central labour laws, many of which were decades old and no longer suited to the present economy.

The consolidation aims to simplify compliance, strengthen worker protection, and bring labour regulation in line with current industry needs.

Key Changes in the Four Labour Codes
1. Code on Wages (2019)

This code brings uniformity to wage-related rules:

• Introduces a national floor wage applicable across sectors.

• Redefines “wages,” which impacts calculation of bonuses and social-security benefits.

• Makes overtime pay mandatory at twice the regular wage rate.

• Requires employers to issue appointment letters to all workers.

2. Industrial Relations Code (2020)

This code reshapes workplace relations and dispute handling:

• Raises the layoff/retrenchment approval threshold from 100 to 300 employees.

• Formally introduces fixed-term employment for needs-based hiring.

• Updates mechanisms for dispute resolution, strikes, and conciliation.

3. Code on Social Security (2020)

This code expands social protection:

• Extends coverage to gig workers, platform workers, and fixed-term

employees. • Allows fixed-term employees to receive gratuity after one year of service.

• Enables portability of social-security benefits across jobs and states.

• Introduces a reskilling fund for retrenched workers.

4. OSHWC Code (2020)

This code strengthens workplace safety and welfare:

• Combines various older laws into a single, unified framework.

• Allows women to work night shifts with safeguards and consent.

• Requires free annual health check-ups for eligible workers.

• Introduces single registration, single licensing, and single return for compliance.

Who Benefits From These Reforms?
Workers / Employees

• Greater formalisation: Mandatory appointment letters and clear wage definitions improve transparency.

• Better wage protection: A national floor wage ensures a minimum benchmark nationwide.

• Improved social security: Gig, platform, and fixed-term workers receive structured benefits for the first time.

• Stronger safety measures: Annual health check-ups and updated safety rules improve workplace well-being.

Employers / Businesses

1. Simplified compliance: Four comprehensive codes replace 29 laws.

2. Flexible workforce management: Higher layoff thresholds and fixed-term hiring help meet changing industry demands.

3. Reduced paperwork: Single registration and return reduce bureaucratic complexity.

4. Clarity in cost planning: Standardised definitions of wages help with long-term workforce planning.

Challenges & Concerns

Despite the potential, several issues need careful handling:

1. Implementation Delays

Central rules are in place, but many state-level rules may take time, slowing full execution.

2. Awareness Among Workers

Informal and gig workers may not immediately understand their new rights.

3. Cost Pressure on SMEs

Must hold Board Meetings and Annual General Meetings .

Smaller businesses may face higher wage and compliance costs in the short term.

4. Enforcement Capacity

Authorities need strong digital systems and trained inspectors to handle new responsibilities effectively.

5. Opposition From Unions

Some unions argue the codes favour employers, particularly the relaxed layoff thresholds.

Strategic Impact on Stakeholders
For the Government

• Speed up state rule notifications.

• Strengthen inspector training and digital platforms.

• Run awareness campaigns for workers.

For Businesses / HR Teams

• Update employment letters, contracts, and internal policies.

• Revisit payroll structures as per new wage definitions.

• Train compliance and HR teams.

• Communicate changes to employees clearly.

For Workers

• Understand rights regarding wages, social security, and working conditions.

• Request appointment letters and clarity on wage structure.

• Use available grievance mechanisms.

• Explore eligibility for gratuity and other benefits.

Why This Reform Matters

Modernisation: Streamlines 29 outdated laws into four clear codes.

Wider Social Security: Covers millions of gig and platform workers.

Greater Formalisation: Brings transparency to hiring and wage structures.

Ease of Doing Business: Simplified compliance encourages investment.

Improved Worker Protections: Focus on safety, dignity, and welfare.

The implementation of the four Labour Codes marks a significant turning point in India’s labour landscape. By replacing old, fragmented laws with a modern and unified structure, the reform aims to balance worker welfare with economic growth.

Its success will depend on timely state-level adoption, awareness among workers, and strong execution. If effectively implemented, these reforms can support a more productive workforce, improve job security, and contribute to India’s long-term economic goals.
